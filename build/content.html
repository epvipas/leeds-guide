📜 A short history of Leeds

A market town on the Aire. Leeds grew from a small settlement where the old road crossed the River Aire. Kirkstall Abbey was founded by Cistercian monks in 1152, and by the Middle Ages Briggate was a planned market street of long, narrow burgage plots. Those plots are why the city's famous yards and arcades are so long and thin.

Wool and cloth. From the 1600s Leeds was the market for Yorkshire woollen cloth, traded in vast cloth halls. In the 1700s and 1800s steam-powered mills like Armley Mills, once the largest woollen mill in the world, made it an industrial giant, joined by flax, engineering and ready-made clothing.

Victorian splendour. The new wealth paid for Cuthbert Brodrick's Town Hall (1858) and Corn Exchange (1864), and for the ornate shopping arcades off Briggate. In 1884 Michael Marks opened a penny bazaar stall in Kirkgate Market, and it grew into Marks & Spencer. Louis Le Prince filmed what are believed to be the world's first moving pictures on Leeds Bridge in 1888.

Today Leeds is the North's big financial and legal centre and a huge student city. It is famous for its music venues, its independent food and beer scene, its galleries, and the great sporting grounds of Headingley and Elland Road.

🛍️ The arcades and yards

Leeds has the finest set of Victorian shopping arcades in the North. They were built over the long, thin medieval plots running off Briggate, which is also why the city's oldest pubs hide down narrow yards like Turks Head Yard. Walk them in one go and you'll see marble, mosaic, wrought iron and stained glass.

🍽️ Yorkshire on a plate

  • Yorkshire pudding is eaten as a starter with gravy in the traditional way.
  • Parkin: a sticky ginger and oatmeal cake eaten around Bonfire Night.
  • Yorkshire curd tart and Wensleydale cheese with fruit cake.
  • Tetley's was brewed in Leeds for nearly 200 years. Its art deco old headquarters is now The Tetley bar and restaurant.
